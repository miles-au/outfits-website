Stop overpacking clothes

The extra shirts are not for the weather. They are for indecision.

Most people overpack clothes because they pack items, not looks. A “maybe” tee has no day attached, so it comes along. Then it never leaves the bag.

Pack looks, not maybes

Before you pull things off hangers, decide what you will wear on Tuesday. Then Wednesday. If a piece is not in one of those looks, it does not earn a spot.

Repeats are the feature

The same trousers on three days is how a carry-on works. Backup outfits that look like the first outfits are how a bag gets heavy.

Backup outfits that look like the first outfits are how a bag gets heavy.

Let the list be strict

Your packing list should be the unique pieces across the planned looks. Not the closet. Not “what if we go out.” If a new plan appears, add a look, then add the item.

Weather belongs on the day, not in the maybe pile

A rain layer is for the day it rains. It is one piece. It is not an excuse to bring a second casual wardrobe.

What this looks like in Outfits

Plan a look for each day of the trip. Wear counts show what you will actually use. The packing list only includes those pieces, so the bag matches the week.
